Bring-up Notes

The whole process from getting from an assembled board to a working one.

How I troubleshoot

1. Isolate

Cut the problem down to the smallest piece of the board that still misbehaves, and give it an input I control: a current-limited supply, or a known voltage injected in place of the real source.

2. Trace

Follow the signal from that input through the circuit one stage at a time, checking each measurement against what the schematic says should be there.

3. Track down

The fault is at the first stage where the measurement and the expectation disagree. From there I work out which part is responsible, sometimes by removing suspects one at a time, and replace it.

HVC Precharge Issue

Board: High Voltage Controller prototype

The issue. On the first prototype, precharge wasn't coming on when we expected it to.

Keeping it low voltage. The real circuit works against a 556 V pack, which is not something to have live on the bench while hunting a fault. So we injected a voltage across the precharge resistors from a bench supply to simulate a precharge, which let us do all of the probing at low voltage.

Following the signal. With a known voltage going in, we traced it through the analog comparator circuit one stage at a time. The voltage at the comparator's input was what we expected. We then probed the comparator's output and the optocoupler after it, and found the optocoupler was not passing the right signal along.

The fix. The optocoupler was defective. We replaced it.

What I took from it. Start from an input you control and check each stage in order. The fault is at the first stage whose output is wrong, and everything before it is cleared along the way.
