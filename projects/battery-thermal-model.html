Tractive Battery Thermal Model

This project proposes a MATLAB model that predicts cell temperature and state of charge across a full FSAE endurance run, to judge whether a theoretical Molicel P50B pack needs active cooling. All data used is sourced from Molicel's published data and lapsim power requirement simulations, and allows us to accurately predict cooling requirements before testing and assembling a battery pack.

Tools
MATLAB
Cell
Molicel P50B
Pack
Theoretical 140S3P pack
Inputs
LapSim power data over an endurance run, published DCIR vs SoC and cell voltage vs SoC data
Status
Model built, working on building testbench for in-house data collection and validation

Approach

The model solves a coupled two-state system: cell temperature and state of charge. Per-cell current comes from lapsim power data which gets divided across the entire pack. Internal resistance (DCIR) is looked up as a function of state of charge from Molicel's published P50B data, so resistance rises realistically as the pack drains.

Qgen = I2 R(SOC)    Qloss = hA (T − Tamb)    dSOC/dt = −I / Qcell

This model assumes heat generation is ohmic, and heat rejection is lumped convection to ambient, where hA is the calibration parameter to be fitted against bench data. State of charge comes from coulomb counting and feeds back into the DCIR lookup, closing the loop. The endurance run is built by repeating the lapsim lap profile for all 20 laps.

Results

For the assumed conditions, cell temperature rises from about 30 °C to about 36 °C over the 20-minute run. The steepest climb comes in the first five minutes. State of charge falls almost linearly over the same run.

Limitations

The model is deliberately simple. Each cell is a single lumped thermal mass. Heat generation is ohmic only, ignoring entropic heat. Current is derived from power at a fixed nominal voltage, ignoring voltage sag. DCIR depends on state of charge but not temperature, and the convection term and material properties are typical Li-ion values, not measurements.

Its strength is how little it needs. From a DCIR curve alone it produces useful predictions before a physical cell exists, which makes it a practical screening tool for comparing candidate cells before any bench testing. The next step is measuring DCIR and temperature rise on a real cell to calibrate hA and validate the model.
