Tractive Battery Thermal Model

This project proposes a MATLAB model that predicts cell temperature and state of charge across a full FSAE endurance run, to judge whether a theoretical Molicel P50B pack needs active cooling. All inputs come from Molicel's published data and lapsim power simulations, so we can estimate cooling requirements before testing and assembling a battery pack.

Tools
MATLAB
Cell
Molicel P50B
Pack
Theoretical 140S3P pack
Inputs
LapSim power data over an endurance run, published DCIR vs SoC and cell voltage vs SoC data
Status
Model built, working on building testbench for in-house data collection and validation

Approach

The model solves a coupled two-state system: cell temperature and state of charge. Lapsim pack power is split evenly across all 420 cells of the 140S3P pack, with regen ignored, and converted to per-cell current using the sagged terminal voltage (see below). Internal resistance (DCIR) is looked up as a function of state of charge from Molicel's published P50B data.

mcpdTdt=Qgen−Qloss
Qgen=I2R(SOC)
Qloss=hA(T−Tamb)
dSOCdt=−IQcell

The model assumes heat generation is ohmic and heat rejection is lumped convection to ambient. The specific heat cp and the convection term hA are the calibration parameters to be fitted against bench data. State of charge comes from coulomb counting against a cell capacity of 4.6 Ah, Molicel's rated capacity at a 60 W discharge rather than the 5 Ah nominal, and feeds back into the DCIR and voltage lookups, closing the loop. The endurance run is built by repeating the lapsim lap profile for all 20 laps, and the system is integrated with MATLAB's ode45.

Voltage sag

Dividing power by a fixed nominal voltage underestimates current, because terminal voltage drops under load and as the cell drains. Instead, the model looks up open-circuit voltage from Molicel's published voltage vs. SoC curve and treats the cell as that voltage source in series with its DCIR:

V=Voc(SOC)−IR(SOC)
Pcell=VI

Solving these together for terminal voltage gives:

V=Voc+Voc2−4PcellR2
I=PcellV

This is solved at every ODE time step, so current, and with it I2R heating, climbs on high-power sections of the lap and toward the end of the run, when Voc is low and R is high.

Results

For the assumed conditions, cell temperature rises from about 30 °C to 42.5 °C over the 22-minute run. The temperature rises rapidly during the start and end of the run. This makes sense as DCIR is higher when the cell is full or almost depleted, and lowest when the cell is at a moderate state of charge. State of charge falls almost linearly over the same run.

Limitations

The model is deliberately simple, but that simplicity gives us a clear baseline to test and validate our data against. Each cell is a single lumped thermal mass, and heat generation is still ohmic only, ignoring entropic heat. It now accounts for voltage sag, but DCIR depends on state of charge and not temperature, current is shared evenly across every cell, and regen is ignored. The specific heat (900 J/kg·K) and convection term (hA = 0.2 W/K) are typical Li-ion values, not measurements.

Its strength is how little data it needs. Every input comes from data publicly available on the manufacturer's website, so it produces useful predictions before a physical cell exists. The next step is measuring the specific heat capacity cp and the convection term hA on a real cell to calibrate and validate the model.
